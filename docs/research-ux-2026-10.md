# Fix the phone edit loop first

The most valuable next work on this deckbuilder is not a new feature but a phone edit loop the household can trust: every one-tap edit validated and undoable, every deck opening on a searchable text list instead of fifteen screens of card art, and one ownership line ("2 owned · 1 in Iron Fortress · 1 free") wherever a card appears. Two independent bodies of evidence point to that order. Commander players' most repeated requests to Moxfield and Archidekt — which deck holds each physical copy, a real undo, one card pool shared by several people — are things this app's database already models and Archidekt's staff say they cannot check on the fly ([Archidekt forum](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/15420664)), yet the phone UI shows that data as deck-file slugs at the bottom of a sheet. Meanwhile a hands-on audit of the running app found edit flows that silently damage decks: one tap swapped a white enchantment into a mono-green deck, "+ Add card" filed Cultivate as a second commander in a 101-card deck, the ⚡ Optimize report vanished with its errors swallowed, and the Optimize confirm promises "every change is revertable" though no undo exists (audit flows). By estimate, about a week of extra-small fixes ends the known ways the app corrupts a deck or misstates a fact; three medium projects — undo, the phone deck page and ownership everywhere — then close the largest gaps, and plain gzip compression alone would shrink the 1.23 MB collection page to 117 KB. Archidekt's 2026 mobile overhaul, which added a tap per card and drew "Is there a way to roll back?" replies, shows how to ship: in small slices, with old paths kept reachable and taps counted before and after ([Archidekt news](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)). The evidence has limits — no competitor page could be opened directly, Reddit was unreachable, the audit ran in phone emulation without card images or a member login, and nobody watched the two household members use the app — so a thirty-minute observed session with them would outrank any further desk research.

## Players' loudest unmet wishes already live in this app's data

The competitive picture favours this app. The requests that recur most on Moxfield's feedback board and Archidekt's forum, and that neither platform has solved, concern physical cards rather than polish. **At least thirteen Moxfield threads** ask to see which deck holds a copy, to hide cards already in use or to enforce "strict assignment" ([nolt /776](https://moxfield.nolt.io/776); [nolt /2249](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2249); [nolt /2263](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2263)), and **at least eight Archidekt threads** ask the same. Archidekt's staff explain why they can't deliver: collection and deck records are "totally independent", so a deck can claim more copies than you own and still read "Owned", and checking every deck against the collection "would take too long to run on the fly" ([Archidekt forum 15420664](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/15420664)). Players have built their own workarounds. MTG Collection Lens shows "which of your decks it's currently sitting in" and advertises that "Your data stays in your browser" ([Firefox Add-ons](https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/mtg-collection-lens/)), and Fetch MTG's "missing-card locator" does the same ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/fetch-mtg)).

Undo forms the second cluster, with **seven threads on Moxfield's board alone**, where the existing rollback is called cumbersome because "every change you roll back adds another entry to the history" ([nolt /1914](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1914); [nolt /1479](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1479); [nolt /1718](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1718)). Declared rather than imposed brackets ([nolt /1859](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1859); [nolt /1861](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1861)) and pools shared by several people ([nolt /1769](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1769); [Archidekt forum 403378](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/403378)) complete the list. This app already holds the data model for every one of these wishes — one SQLite collection with a change ledger, every deck revision stored, pins, cross-deck conflict detection and named household members ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md); [store.py](../scripts/store.py)) — so, as the table shows, the gap is presentation rather than data.

| Unmet wish (signal strength) | Why the big platforms struggle | What this app already has | What a phone user sees today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which deck holds each copy (strong: 13+ Moxfield, 8+ Archidekt threads) | Separate collection and deck ledgers; too slow to check on the fly | One collection database, `deck_conflicts`, pins, a Shared page | Deck-file slugs joined by " · ", not links, starting 945 px down an 812 px-tall sheet |
| Undo and version restore (strong: 7 Moxfield threads) | Each rollback adds a history entry; deleted decks need a support email | Every revision stored, a ledger, `.changes.csv` | No undo, revert or restore anywhere |
| Declare a bracket and see the evidence (strong) | Labels auto-assigned from Game Changer counts | Declared and detected shown side by side ("your setting (detected 2)") | Already the right model |
| One pool, several people (moderate) | One account per collection; shared copies break the warnings | Named members, labelled edits, an activity log | Slugs, and raw log verbs such as "replace card" |
| Native-feeling, offline phone use (moderate) | No native apps; installable web only | Installed PWA with fingerprinted assets | Deck and collection pages need a live connection |

The right-hand column comes from the audit's measurements and code reading (audit flows; [cardpanel.js](../webapp/static/cardpanel.js); [sw.js](../webapp/static/sw.js)).

The leaders' best interaction ideas are small and cheap to borrow. Reviewers praise Moxfield because its "deck builder is the cleanest to use, and it's on the same screen as the deck view page" ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/best-mtg-deck-builder/)), with `/` to focus search and Shift+Enter to add the top result ([Moxfield shortcuts](https://moxfield.com/help/shortcuts)). Archidekt contributes the most. Its card-details overlay gained tabs listing the other decks a card is in, fetched one card at a time "to improve load times" ([Archidekt Collection V2++](https://archidekt.com/news/6801001)); its quick add confirms with a toast naming the card and its destination, "Added Counterspell to Removal" ([Archidekt forum 19986131](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/19986131)); clicking a chart element highlights the matching cards and fades the rest ([Archidekt Deck Stats](https://archidekt.com/news/9287631)); and its mid-2026 phone layout puts **Search · Quick add · Layout · Undo · More** in a bottom toolbar ([Archidekt mobile overhaul](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)). EDHREC's redesign of 2026-10-13, justified by "about half of you are browsing EDHREC on your phones", shows two cards per row with a card-size toggle ([EDHREC changelog](https://edhrec.com/articles/changelog-edhrec-redesign)), and ManaBox's Missing section separates partly missing from missing cards and copies every missing card to a list with one long-press ([ManaBox guide](https://www.manabox.app/guides/decks/missing-cards/)). Across all of them, four views — Text, Condensed, Stacks and Grid — with Group and Sort controls that work independently of the view are now what a player arriving from Moxfield or Archidekt expects ([Moxfield wiki](https://github.com/moxfield/moxfield-public/wiki/Features)).

Their failures teach as much. When Archidekt moved Quick Add into a drawer, users answered within days that adding 20 cards had become "tiresome", that one "struggled initially to locate the playtest button" now hidden under More, and that taps registered "entire inches below where im actually tapping"; Archidekt shipped a toggle to keep the drawer open, which one user called "a lifesaver" ([Archidekt mobile overhaul](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)). Its 2024 split of one mana chart into many small ones drew enough pushback that "The pie chart has returned!" ([Archidekt Deck Stats](https://archidekt.com/news/9287631)). Moxfield users find card images "so tiny I can't read the card on my phone" ([nolt /2400](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2400)), lose sight of a card while tagging it ([nolt /2347](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2347)) and call automatic bracket labels an ultimatum ([nolt /1859](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1859)), and a May 2026 ranking of deck testers marks Moxfield down because its tester "doesn't support mulligans" ([Draftsim testers](https://draftsim.com/mtg-deck-tester/)). The collection apps' worst stories concern data loss and wrong prices: ManaBox's own FAQ warns that turning on sync on a device with incomplete data can leave a collection that "appears empty" ([ManaBox sync FAQ](https://manabox.app/guides/sync/faq/)), TopDecked reviewers report "the latest version of decklists getting lost" to failed syncs ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/topdecked-mtg/id1173388234?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)), and scanners that default to "the most recent or most common printing" put the wrong price on the right card ([scanyourmtg](https://www.scanyourmtg.com/review/tcgplayer/)).

Several popular features are wrong for a free, self-hosted, two-person app and should be declined on purpose. Camera scanning, printing, foil and condition fields, and physical binders cause most of the category's accuracy complaints while serving goals — set completion, resale value — that this household does not have, and the app's name-level model avoids that whole class of error. Value-over-time charts, winners-and-losers lists and price alerts need a price history the app does not keep, and its prices are estimates by rule. A life counter would join a field Draftsim ranked twelve deep in August 2026, where the winners do the player's arithmetic, such as commander damage that "automatically ticks down life totals" ([Draftsim life counters](https://draftsim.com/best-mtg-life-counter-app/)). And "AI builds your deck" framing is a liability: an older Star City Games test produced an 83-card "100-card" list with doubled singletons ([Star City Games](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/ai-brewed-me-two-commander-decklists/)), one Archidekt deck is titled "i will now refer to uninspired decks as 'ai generated'" ([Archidekt deck](https://archidekt.com/decks/4332485)), and DeckCheck, the analysis tool that drew a warmer reception, pitched itself as "not trying to replace human creativity" ([DeckCheck](https://deckcheck.co/blog/about)). The app's grounding rules already follow the trust rules players name — never invent a card, explain each suggestion, keep estimates honest — so its wording should say "suggestions from field data and your collection", not "AI".

One caution covers this whole section. No competitor page could be opened from the research sandbox, so the competitor and player evidence comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages; Reddit was unreachable even as snippets; and many 2026 hands-on scanner verdicts come from scanyourmtg.com, which a rival scanner publishes. Thread counts measure how often a wish recurs, not how many people share it. The recommendations rely on the direction of the evidence, which is consistent across platforms and years.

## Four edit flows can corrupt decks or lose information silently

The audit's most serious findings are invisible in screenshots. They are state bugs that appear only when a flow runs end to end, and they fall in the class the repo's own test policy ranks first: code that can "destroy data or lie" ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). **Replace commits on a single tap, with no confirmation and no colour-identity check.** In mono-green Beorn, the audit opened Hulking Raptor, tapped "Replace…", typed "Sol" and tapped the first result, which wrote SOLDIER Military Program, a white enchantment, into the deck with no dialog, no error and no message beyond a NEW badge (audit flow F2). The cause spans both layers. The panel asks `confirm()` only for Remove and calls `editCard('replace', …)` straight from search results and "Stronger options" ([card_panel.html](../scripts/assets/card_panel.html)), while the server's replace route rejects only duplicates, even though the add route's `_validate_add` already refuses cards "outside this deck's color identity" ([app.py](../webapp/app.py)). Calling `_validate_add` from the replace route, and filtering replace-search results to the deck's colours as Moxfield's search has long done so that illegal cards never appear ([Moxfield wiki](https://github.com/moxfield/moxfield-public/wiki/Features)), closes the hole in under a day.

Three more flows fail in the same quiet way. **"+ Add card" preselects "Commander" as the section**, because the picker lists sections in deck-file order: the audit added Cultivate without touching the dropdown, and the dashboard then read "Commander 2" and "Total 101" with no warning ([add_card.html](../scripts/assets/add_card.html); audit flows). The default should be the card's own type section, which `deck_sections.py` already computes, and any write that leaves a deck at other than 100 cards or one commander should say so. **The ⚡ Optimize report never reaches the player.** The route flashes a summary and redirects to the generated dashboard, which renders no flash messages, and it wraps the whole run in `except Exception: pass`, so a failed run is silent; in the audit the summary and three "Held: …" notes resurfaced later as five stale messages on the Decks page ([app.py](../webapp/app.py); audit flow F9). Finally, **both Optimize confirms promise that "every change is revertable"** ([index.html](../webapp/templates/index.html); [build_dashboard.py](../scripts/build_dashboard.py)), yet no route or template offers revert, restore or undo, although the Collection page reports "every version (50 so far)" stored and `store.restore(…, rev=)` already exists ([store.py](../scripts/store.py); audit collection screenshot).

A further group of bugs misstates facts, several of them on the most public screens. The Rule-0 table card — the screen a player hands to opponents — shows raw markdown such as "Five-color \*\*dragons\*\*", renders the heading "The Plan" as a bullet and splits one sentence across two bullets, because `_plan_lines` takes the first six raw lines of the deck notes and strips only leading symbols; the Plan tab turns every hard-wrapped line into its own paragraph and leaves `*italic*` asterisks showing ([app.py](../webapp/app.py); [build_dashboard.py](../scripts/build_dashboard.py); audit table-card screenshot). The Ur-Dragon reads "combat T9" on its deck card and dashboard but "Speed unmeasured (no combat clock)" on Assess, because the Assess route calls `deckcore.analyze_deck` without the collection path that feeds the goldfish clock — a bug class already fixed once on the leaderboard ([app.py](../webapp/app.py)). The mulligan trainer recorded Keep, Keep, Mulligan on one hand as "3 hand(s) this device · you and the sim agreed on 67%", because `.mull-actions{display:flex}` overrides the `hidden` attribute ([mulligan.html](../webapp/templates/mulligan.html); audit flows); one global `[hidden]{display:none !important}` rule retires that class of bug everywhere. The Wishlist shows 85 checkboxes with no label, name, script or persistence, and its totals read "~$0.00" when prices are unknown, where the grounding rules call for "unknown" ([wishlist.html](../webapp/templates/wishlist.html); audit axe). And the hosted app still describes itself as a local one: the footer says "your collection stays on your machine", the offline page asks whether "that PC is awake, `run.bat` is running", the delete confirm points to git, and the batch preview names `owned_additions.txt` even in database mode ([base.html](../webapp/templates/base.html); [sw.js](../webapp/static/sw.js); [batch_preview.html](../webapp/templates/batch_preview.html)). The audit traces this wording, and the slugs discussed below, to one root cause: screens written for the command line or the developer, reused unchanged in a household app.

### Undo is cheap here because the history already exists

Undo is the safety net that makes one-tap phone edits acceptable. Practitioner consensus — not a controlled study — holds that reversible actions should take effect at once and offer undo, with confirmation reserved for destructive ones, because frequent "Are you sure?" prompts train people to click through ([W3C cognitive-accessibility list](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-cognitive-a11y-tf/2020Apr/0059.html)). Apple adds that an undo should describe its result, show the restored item, allow several steps and may revert a batch of related changes at once ([Apple HIG: Undo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/undo-and-redo)). WCAG constrains the toast that carries it: its message must be announced through a status role without taking focus ([Understanding 4.1.3](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/21/status-messages.html)), and an auto-dismissing message escapes the timing criterion only if the same function exists somewhere else ([Understanding 2.2.1](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/20/timing-adjustable.html)). The design that follows is a toast such as "Replaced Hulking Raptor with Cultivate · Undo", shown for at least ten seconds and paused while focused, backed by a permanent Undo on each row of the deck's Changes page and a "Restore this version" action over the stored revisions, with a whole ⚡ run undone as one batch. Each undo should be a new inverse edit written through `deckcore.append_changes` with a manual source, so the optimizer still never churns a deliberate choice, and the Changes page should fold an edit and its undo into one struck-through line — an honest ledger without the cluttered history Moxfield users resent. Confirmation dialogs then shrink to the four destructive operations — replacing the collection by upload, deleting a deck, restoring a backup and removing a member — each with a verb-labelled button, a default-focused Cancel and a plain statement of the consequence.

The optimizer needs visibility more than redesign. Running it automatically in four places is a deliberate, documented design, the ⚡ button is an explicit request, and its guardrails — a 25-point field-gain threshold, never cutting cards named in the notes, featured in clips or swapped in by hand — are exactly the restraint players ask for ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). The evidence objects to opacity: on Moxfield's request for automatic optimization to a target bracket, commenters preferred a tool that proposes swaps for approval over one that decides ([nolt /1847](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1847)), and today the app shows neither its swaps nor its reasons for holding cards. Showing the result on the dashboard (Wave 0) and making a run undoable as a unit (Wave 1) satisfy that evidence without touching the design. A preview sheet listing each proposed swap with its reason, field percentage and fit, each approved or skipped, would go further at little cost, since `optimize.py` already previews unless told to apply; but it changes a deliberate, documented behaviour, so it belongs on the player's decision list rather than in a spec.

### Every one of these defects passed the existing quality gate

That fact matters more than any single fix. The UAT sweep printed "no overflow or small-target flags" while the Pins page's only action sat clipped off-screen at 412 px and the Power tab's breakdown was cut mid-word ("10 removal/("), because the sweep skips anything inside an `overflow:auto` parent or the panel overlays and flags only targets under 36 px ([sweep.js](../webapp/uat/sweep.js); audit sweep log). The audit also found nearly all of today's phone problems to be new since the August–October specs, arising from later features or from flows nobody exercised end to end. A flow-level UAT step — scripted replace, add, optimize and mulligan runs that read the deck file back, modelled on the audit's own `flows.js` — together with a sweep that fails below WCAG's 24 px floor, warns below 44 px and includes overlays and scrolling containers, is the process change that stops the next refresh from regressing the way an installed phone once painted new markup with old CSS.

## The phone deck page must open on a list, not fifteen screens of art

The deck page is the centre of the app, and on a phone it hides its own navigation. At 412×915 px the sticky tab bar begins **1,154 px down, or 1.26 screens**, beneath five summary tiles, five role meters and a "Print / Save as PDF" button, and on Tergrid's seven tabs "Plan" and "More" end at x = 451 and 521 px, past the screen edge with no partial label to hint they exist (audit metrics; [build_dashboard.py](../scripts/build_dashboard.py)). The Deck tab is a two-up image grid **14.1–14.8 screens** long and the Plan tab runs to 25; there is no in-deck search and no list view, so the last of Beorn's 71 unique cards sits 11.9 screens down (audit flows). The deck-subtabs spec's own acceptance test, "Buy is reachable in one tap from deck open, no scrolling", has failed since the range meters went in above the tabs ([spec-deck-subtabs.md](../docs/spec-deck-subtabs.md)). The arithmetic favours text: at the 30 px row height a Moxfield-imitating app measured on Moxfield ([MTG CardVault release notes](https://github.com/jflockton/mtg-cardvault-releases/releases/tag/v0.21.0)), a 100-card list runs about 3,000 px, roughly 3.5 phone screens, against the fifteen the grid takes — our estimate, though the audit's measured grid confirms its grid side.

The case for keeping art first deserves a hearing: players recognise cards by their illustrations, Archidekt sells itself as the visual builder, and EDHREC has just added an images-only view ([EDHREC changelog](https://edhrec.com/articles/changelog-edhrec-redesign)). On a phone, though, a grid serves browsing rather than reviewing a hundred cards, and a thumbnail in every row keeps the recognition. The phone layout should therefore open on a one-line summary — count, bracket, clock and the worst role shortfall — that expands into the tiles, with the tab bar inside the first screen and every tab visible through shorter labels or an edge fade. The Deck tab should default to a text list of 48–56 px rows, each carrying a `small` 146×204 Scryfall thumbnail ([Scryfall imagery](https://scryfall.com/docs/api/images)), the name, the mana cost and an ownership mark, under collapsible `<details>` section headers with card counts, which also answers Archidekt users' most repeated layout request, collapsing finished stacks ([Archidekt forum 7537520](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/7537520)). A filter box above the list should narrow rows as the player types; client-side Group by (section, role, mana value, colour) and Sort should reorder rows from data attributes already in the page; and the chosen view, grouping and collapsed sections should persist per viewer in `localStorage`, since Archidekt users notice when "'Stack By' and 'View As' preferences" fail to stick ([Archidekt forum 2975142](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/2975142/1)). Tapping a row opens the card panel, and tapping the panel's image opens a full-screen, pinch-zoomable `large` image, the fix Moxfield users request by pointing at Archidekt ([nolt /2400](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2400)). Tapping a mana-curve bar or a colour's pip count should filter the list to those cards, borrowing Archidekt's chart interaction instead of adding charts ([Archidekt Deck Stats](https://archidekt.com/news/9287631)), and the image grid should stay one tap away as a remembered alternative view.

The card panel needs reordering more than redesign. On a phone its art fills the first screen of the sheet, "In your decks" begins 945 px down an 812 px-tall sheet, the close button measures 30×33 px in the app and 34×35 px on the dashboard, and the paired-games swap simulation is reachable only by Shift-click, which no phone can do (audit metrics; [card_panel.html](../scripts/assets/card_panel.html); [cardpanel.css](../webapp/static/cardpanel.css)). The phone also fetches oracle text live from `api.scryfall.com` every time the panel opens ([cardpanel.js](../webapp/static/cardpanel.js)). On phones the panel should lead with the name, the ownership line and the actions, then the card text, then the art; it should offer "Simulate first" as a visible button beside each candidate, grow its close target to 44 px and keep the image in view while the player edits, the complaint behind Moxfield's /2347 ([nolt /2347](https://moxfield.nolt.io/2347)). Rebuilding both panels on `<dialog>` with `showModal()`, Baseline widely available since 2024-09-14 ([web-features 3.42.0](https://registry.npmjs.org/web-features/-/web-features-3.42.0.tgz)), fixes the audit's focus findings in the same change. Pushing a history entry when the sheet opens lets Android's back gesture close it before leaving the page, which matters because in an installed PWA that gesture otherwise walks browser history ([Discourse thread](https://meta.discourse.org/t/back-button-in-ios-pwa/93909?page=5)).

The shell around the deck is sound but stops at the deck's door. The phone bottom bar — Decks, Build, Collection, Wishlist and a labelled More — sits inside the three-to-five destinations Apple and Android recommend ([Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars); [Android navigation bar](https://developer.android.google.cn/develop/ui/compose/components/navigation-bar?hl=ca)), and although Apple warns that a More overflow makes hidden sections harder to reach, that is tolerable here because Shared, Pins, People and setup are rarely used. Apple also insists the tab bar stay visible in every section, which is exactly what the dashboard breaks: it has its own app bar, no bottom bar on phones and no rail on desktop, so "← All decks" and the browser's Back are its only exits (audit metrics; [build_dashboard.py](../scripts/build_dashboard.py)). Bringing the dashboard inside the shell fixes three problems in one move: the missing navigation, the two different "⋯" menus (Home's has Delete but no Changes, the dashboard's has Changes but no Delete) and the flash messages the dashboard never renders ([index.html](../webapp/templates/index.html)). Deck sub-pages need real titles and back links — mulligan and table card carry the generic title "MTG Deckbuilder", mulligan and the visual page have no back link, and the table card's sits 1.6 screens down — which matters twice over because iOS home-screen apps reportedly lack the edge-swipe back gesture ([Discourse thread](https://meta.discourse.org/t/back-button-in-ios-pwa/93909?page=5)). Pages reachable only as buttons on other pages, such as Activity (reachable only from the owner-only People page), Import, New arrivals and Changes, belong in the More sheet, and the one-time "Phone" setup page should give up its permanent slot there. The largest navigation gain is a global search: a header icon opening a full-screen sheet over the existing owned-card search, which answers in about 10 ms, with the ownership line on every result row. Today, "do I own it, and which deck has it?" costs two taps, 3.3 screens of scrolling to reach the collection's search box and another 1.2 screens inside the card sheet (audit flows; audit timings).

The remaining friction is wording and density. "Click any card" and "Shift-click" instructions appear on touch screens, and some meaning lives only in `title=` tooltips a phone cannot show — the share badge's "also in", the role-meter targets on home cards and the definition of "Ready to sleeve" ([build_dashboard.py](../scripts/build_dashboard.py); [index.html](../webapp/templates/index.html)). The Decks home fits about one deck per screen: each card is 358 px tall, twelve decks take 6.8 screens at 412 px, and at 360 px not one card is fully visible above the bottom bar (audit home metrics). A compact two-line row would show three or four decks per screen; "Ready to sleeve", which today means only that all 100 cards are owned and appears beside a "Draw 0, short" warning on Bartolomé, should be renamed to what it measures; and the "↻ Rebuild wishlist + static dashboards" developer button belongs off the main page. Build Next pushes its first result 801 px down, behind a six-line legend and 24 archetype chips that wrap into seven rows, each chip a full page load where Home's chips filter instantly, and its commander autocomplete calls Scryfall from the phone and fails silently — typing "Doctor" produced no suggestions and no message ([build_next.html](../webapp/templates/build_next.html); audit metrics). Suggestions should come through the server, which can reach Scryfall and knows the owned cards, rendered with the W3C's ARIA combobox pattern and a visible "suggestions unavailable" state; `<datalist>` is no substitute, since it is not Baseline and its options can be neither styled nor zoomed ([APG combobox](https://github.com/w3c/aria-practices/blob/main/content/patterns/combobox/combobox-pattern.html); [MDN datalist](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/html/reference/elements/datalist/index.md)).

The audit's tap counts give a baseline that any redesign should beat (audit flows; audit metrics):

| Core flow at 412 px | Today | Target after Waves 0–1 |
|---|---|---|
| Open a deck and read one card | 2 taps plus 1.7–11.9 screens of scrolling; card text needs a live Scryfall call from the phone | 2 taps plus a few letters typed in the deck filter; card text served by the app |
| Swap one card | 4 taps; committed instantly, off-colour allowed, no undo | 4 taps; colour-checked; a toast with Undo |
| Find which deck holds a card | 2 taps, 3.3 screens to the search box, about 1.2 screens inside the sheet; the answer comes as slugs | Search icon, a few letters, read the result row |
| Paste three cards into the collection | 4 taps after 1.36 screens of scrolling | 4 taps, no scrolling; Add cards first on the page |
| Check the wishlist | 1 tap, then 19.7 screens of tables | 1 tap; grouped by deck and collapsed |

### Accessibility debt is narrow: labels, focus and targets

The foundations are strong, and any refresh must keep them. axe-core found **zero colour-contrast violations on every page**, body text reaches 14.99:1 and muted text 7.99:1, every focusable element shows a 2 px focus ring, and primary buttons are 44 px tall with bottom-bar items at 52 px (audit axe; [tokens.css](../scripts/assets/tokens.css); [app.css](../webapp/static/app.css)). The failures are concentrated and mostly quick to fix. Controls lack accessible names: the 85 wishlist checkboxes, three collection filter selects, two quantity inputs and the editor's textarea. Card names on the Wishlist, Shared, Changes, Arrivals, Pins and Activity pages are `<b>` or `<span>` elements with no `tabindex` or role, so a keyboard user can never open the panel from them ([cardpanel.js](../webapp/static/cardpanel.js)). Both panels declare `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, yet they leave focus on the tapped card and drop it to `<body>` on Escape, with no focus trap. Amber links contrast only 1.19:1 and 1.58:1 with the surrounding text and carry no underline; the dashboard has no `main` landmark; there is no skip link, so ten tab stops come before the first button on desktop Home; and because no `color-scheme: dark` is declared, native checkboxes and select pop-ups render white on the dark filters (audit axe).

WCAG 2.2's new level-AA criteria set the remaining bar: targets of at least 24×24 CSS px ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/guidelines/sc/22/target-size-minimum.html)), focused items never completely hidden by the sticky header, sub-tabs or bottom bar, which `scroll-padding` satisfies ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/22/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)), and a single-tap alternative for every drag ([WCAG 2.5.7](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/guidelines/sc/22/dragging-movements.html)). Touch-target tokens such as `--target: 44px` belong in `tokens.css`, which may hold sizes but never colours. The smallest type token, 0.694 rem or about 11.1 px, now carries the bottom-nav labels and table headers at roughly Apple's 11-point floor ([Apple HIG: Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)), and raising it to 12–13 px is a one-line change that both the app and the dashboards inherit. Every input should render at 16 px or more so that iOS does not zoom in on focus, a fix of font size rather than of disabled zoom ([Rick Strahl](https://weblog.west-wind.com/posts/2023/Apr/17/Preventing-iOS-Textbox-Auto-Zooming-and-ViewPort-Sizing)).

## Showing where each copy sits is the category's widest gap

Ownership is the app's strongest asset and its most hidden one. The data to answer "where is this card right now?" already exists — owned count, each deck's claims, pins and over-commitment all flow through `deck_conflicts`, the pins file and the collection database — and the Collection page even writes each card's decks into a `data-decks` attribute on every tile, using it only for a yes-or-no "in a deck" filter ([collection.html](../webapp/templates/collection.html)). Wherever a person reads that data, though, it arrives as file slugs: the panel's "In your decks" joins slugs with " · " and links none of them, a Wishlist "Shared with" cell such as "captain-america-team-leader, kami-of-the-crescent-moon, …" wraps to about fourteen lines at 412 px, and the Shared, Pins and Activity pages do the same ([cardpanel.js](../webapp/static/cardpanel.js); [shared.html](../webapp/templates/shared.html); audit wishlist screenshot). The fix has two parts: one display layer that turns a deck's file name into its title and a link, and one ownership line — "2 owned · 1 in Iron Fortress (pinned) · 1 free" — shown wherever a card appears, from the first line of the card panel to swap candidates, search results, collection tiles and batch-preview rows. ManaBox gives exactly this answer from its "All collection" search ([ManaBox guide](https://manabox.app/guides/decks/collection-decks/)), and Collection Lens users install a browser extension to get it.

Three more borrowings use data the app already holds. A three-state mark on every deck row — ✓ owned and free, ◐ owned but claimed by another named deck, ✗ not owned — extends Moxfield's blue exact-match and grey partial-match checks with the "in use" state its users keep requesting ([nolt /1618](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1618); [nolt /1433](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1433)). A deck header reading "97/100 owned · 3 shared" answers the request for a "70/100 cards owned" count ([nolt /1275](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1275)), and one "add all missing to the wishlist" button replaces ManaBox's long-press "select every missing card" ([ManaBox missing cards](https://www.manabox.app/guides/decks/missing-cards/)). Shared copies should read as information with a next step — "also in the Giada deck; swap before game night" — rather than as a red conflict, since even Archidekt's staff call insufficient-copy warnings "complicated and database-intensive, especially with edge cases around shared copies" ([Archidekt forum 26797505](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/26797505)). Two new card states can wait for Wave 3: a per-deck proxy flag, which eight Moxfield threads request because Moxfield's proxy tag flips every copy at once ([nolt /1029](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1029); [nolt /1386](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1386)), and a dated "lent to" status that no surveyed tool offers — supported only by players' tag-and-spreadsheet workarounds, and therefore a cheap differentiator rather than a proven demand ([nolt /1027](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1027)).

### The collection page needs search first and a tenth of its weight

The Collection page is the heaviest and most admin-first screen in the app. It ships **1,229,985 bytes of uncompressed HTML, with 11,003 DOM nodes and 3,296 images**, runs to 238 phone screens, and reaches DOMContentLoaded after 1.06 s on a desktop CPU and 2.9 s with the CPU slowed four times; filtering happens in the browser over the whole page, so `/collection?q=dragon` returns the same 1.23 MB (audit network; audit timings). Its first phone screen is entirely a database explainer and download links, followed by a Card DB status card telling the player to "run `enrich.bat`", and the search box begins 2,993 px — 3.3 screens — down, past four separate add-and-edit forms ([collection.html](../webapp/templates/collection.html); audit metrics). Archidekt met the same weight with paging and a page-size selector, an image view, configurable columns and a details overlay, moving its slow "In decks" lookup into the overlay "to improve load times" ([Archidekt Collection V2++](https://archidekt.com/news/6801001)), and for finding one specific card, pagination or an explicit "Load more" beats infinite scrolling ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling-tips)).

The rebuilt page should open on a search form — `type="search"`, a real label, `enterkeyhint="search"` and 16 px text — followed by a "what changed and what needs me" band showing new arrivals not yet in any deck, cards claimed by more decks than there are copies, and recent ledger changes, then one primary Add cards button, with downloads, uploads, merging and enrichment status behind a "Manage" disclosure. Results should be filtered and sorted on the server and sent 60–100 rows at a time behind a real `<a href="?page=2">Load more</a>` link that JavaScript upgrades to an in-place append, with a status line such as "Showing 200 of 3,294 cards" announced without moving focus ([Understanding 4.1.3](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/understanding/21/status-messages.html)). Rows and tiles should take `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size`, Baseline since 2025-09-15, which skips rendering off-screen content while keeping find-in-page, tab order and the accessibility tree intact — all things JavaScript virtualization breaks ([MDN content-visibility](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/css/reference/properties/content-visibility/index.md); [web-features 3.42.0](https://registry.npmjs.org/web-features/-/web-features-3.42.0.tgz)). Frequent filters belong in a chip row covering colour identity, type and location (all, free copies, in a deck, pinned, claimed twice), with the full set in a bottom-sheet `<dialog>` applied by a live "Show 212 cards" button and the applied filters shown as removable chips, an overview that 28–32% of benchmarked shopping sites omit ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/how-to-design-applied-filters)). Every filter should live in the query string so that Back, reload and bookmarks work, where `collection.js` today resets them all ([collection.js](../webapp/static/collection.js)), and a zero-result search should explain itself and offer "did you mean", a Scryfall search and "clear 2 filters" ([NN/g empty states](https://nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design)).

Card intake already leads the category and needs additions, not replacement. Its paste, per-line preview (✓, did-you-mean, ✗, "couldn't be checked") and confirm amount to a scanner's review list done in text, sounder than Archidekt's import, which in an older report turned duplicate lines into separate entries ([Archidekt forum 145906](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/145906/1)), and Moxfield's, which defaults new cards to the newest printing ([Moxfield on X](https://x.com/moxfieldmtg/status/1561375624446582785)). Four additions would make it outstanding: an "already own 2 · in Ur-Dragon" note on each row, merged duplicate lines, a quantity stepper per row, and in-place correction of a ✗ row without re-pasting — the Delver Lens idea of fixing errors without switching modes ([scanyourmtg Delver review](https://www.scanyourmtg.com/review/delver-lens/)). A running header ("42 cards · 3 need a look") and a `localStorage` draft of every paste box would answer the lost-work complaints that dog TCGplayer's app ([scanyourmtg TCGplayer review](https://www.scanyourmtg.com/review/tcgplayer/)), while single-card adds should work the other way round, saving at once behind an Undo rather than a confirm screen. Because data-loss stories are the category's most damaging reviews, the app's real safety — one server record, a ledger of every change, merge-only uploads and daily backups — also deserves one visible line on the Collection page: "Every change is logged · last backup <time>".

## Bytes, fonts and offline copies matter more than Python speed

Server time is not the bottleneck: warm responses take 3–60 ms for most pages and 0.10–0.31 s for deck dashboards, and owned-card search answers in about 10 ms (audit timings; audit deck timings). The cost is bytes. The app compresses nothing even when the browser asks for gzip or brotli; every page loads a render-blocking Google Fonts stylesheet for three families and seven weights, which means 62–82 KB of font files per cold page that the service worker does not cache; and each deck dashboard carries 170–220 KB of inline CSS, JavaScript and per-card data, re-downloaded on every open because the service worker treats deck pages as network-only (audit network; [base.html](../webapp/templates/base.html); [sw.js](../webapp/static/sw.js)). The one slow server path is the first request after the collection's inputs change, which recomputes every deck: `/` took 4.17 s, a cost the audit expects after each sync-triggered reload on PythonAnywhere (audit timings; [mtglib.py](../scripts/mtglib.py)). The audit measured what gzip -9 does to each page with curl (audit directory):

| Page | Raw HTML | After gzip -9 | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/collection` | 1,229,985 B | 117,105 B | 90% |
| `/collection/arrivals` | 308,251 B | 13,582 B | 96% |
| `/deck/the-ur-dragon` | 294,254 B | 56,289 B | 81% |
| `/deck/tergrid-iron-fortress` | 328,441 B | 62,787 B | 81% |
| `/` (Decks home) | 73,793 B | 8,010 B | 89% |

Compression is therefore the first performance change — an `after_request` hook using the standard library's `gzip`, under half a day of work, since the Flask layer may take dependencies even though `scripts/` may not — unless PythonAnywhere's front proxy already compresses, the one thing the audit could not measure. The numbers also rule out a tempting refactor. The audit floated serving the dashboard's inline assets as cacheable static files on the app, but most of that weight is per-deck data no other deck could reuse — a 128,896 B card-data script on the Ur-Dragon, and a 107,818 B card-data script plus a 23,958 B clips file on Tergrid — so externalizing would save only the roughly 50–90 KB of shared CSS and player script, perhaps 10–20 KB once compressed by our estimate, while breaking the repo's rule that a dashboard stays one self-contained file (audit metrics; [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Compress, and leave the rule alone unless field measurements say otherwise. Fonts are the other real win: self-hosting the families as fingerprinted static files through `asset()`, precached by the service worker, removes a cross-origin, render-blocking request from every page. And the slow recompute can be paid by the sync job rather than by the first visitor, by warming the deck analyses right after each sync.

Offline copies are the PWA feature this household would feel most. Deck and collection pages are network-only by design, so a store with weak signal means no deck and no table card ([sw.js](../webapp/static/sw.js)), and Moxfield users argue for a PWA on the same grounds, citing "a lot of offline advantages" ([nolt /1078](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1078); [nolt /1294](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1294)). The fix keeps the existing split, with fingerprinted assets cache-first and pages from the network, and adds navigation preload so the page request starts while the service worker boots ([MDN navigation preload](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/api/navigationpreloadmanager/enable/index.md)), a stored copy of each successful GET page, and — on timeout or when offline — that copy served under a "Saved copy from 14:02 — you're offline" banner, falling back to the offline page. MDN recommends network-first with a cached fallback for responses where "a cached resource is better than nothing" ([MDN PWA caching](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/progressive_web_apps/guides/caching/index.md)), and it is the right strategy for pages the household edits, because stale-while-revalidate would show a stale deck right after an edit, which looks like a lost save. Card text should follow the same logic: the panel's oracle text can come from the server, which reaches Scryfall and keeps a disk cache, through the existing card API rather than a live call from the phone, making it faster and cacheable while card images stay browser hotlinks as the repo requires ([docs/card-images.md](../docs/card-images.md)).

Three browser features make a server-rendered app feel native at almost no cost, and each does nothing in browsers that lack it. Same-origin Speculation Rules prefetch at `moderate` eagerness fetches a page's HTML before the tap completes; it works only in Chromium (Safari 26.2 hides it behind a flag and Firefox lacks it), which suits an Android household, and it must exclude sign-out, exports and any GET with side effects, with Flask skipping activity-log writes for requests that carry `Sec-Purpose: prefetch` ([MDN Speculation Rules](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/api/speculation_rules_api/index.md); [BCD 8.1.5](https://registry.npmjs.org/@mdn/browser-compat-data/-/browser-compat-data-8.1.5.tgz)). Prerender is the wrong trade here, since it would run a whole dashboard and pull up to a hundred Scryfall images for links that may never be tapped. Cross-document view transitions — one `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }` rule inside a `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` query — cross-fade page changes in Chrome 126+ and Safari 18.2+ with no JavaScript ([MDN View Transitions](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/api/view_transition_api/using/index.md)). Keeping pages eligible for the back/forward cache, with no `unload` handlers and no `Cache-Control: no-store` on ordinary pages (a restriction only Chrome has relaxed), makes Back instant ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/bfcache-ccns)), and card images should reserve their space with `width` and `height` and pick among Scryfall's sizes with `srcset`, `small` for list thumbnails and `normal` for grids. A two-person app will never have public Chrome field data, so the way to learn whether any of this helps on the real phone is a self-hosted copy of Google's roughly 3 KB `web-vitals` library, beaconing into a `rum` table in the existing SQLite database: largest contentful paint (when the main content appears), interaction to next paint (how quickly a tap responds), cumulative layout shift (how much the layout jumps) and time to first byte, with the first three judged at the 75th percentile against 2.5 s, 200 ms and 0.1 ([web-vitals README](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/web-vitals/blob/main/README.md)).

### At the table, speed is a social obligation

The table is where the phone is used under the most pressure, and the evidence there is thin but consistent. Players accept pulling up a card on a phone "to present to the pod" ([Commander's Herald](https://commandersherald.com/am-i-the-bolas-playing-signed-cards/)), but etiquette writers ask people to "refrain from going into your phone" between turns ([Hipsters of the Coast, 2022](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/09/top-10-ways-to-make-your-commander-night-a-good-one/)), and one warns that lookups from different sources at one table are "ripe for wasting time" unless the pod agrees on a source beforehand ([Hipsters of the Coast](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/?p=106669)). Rule 0 talk is converging on a declared bracket plus a Game Changer count, which "gets to the point faster" than older power-level conversations ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-brackets/); [Commander's Herald](https://commandersherald.com/commander-brackets-your-deck-is-more-than-a-number/)). The app's table card fits that moment once its markdown is fixed. A full-screen, high-contrast "Show the pod" view should lead with the declared bracket and itemize the evidence beneath it — Game Changers, tutors, fast mana, combos and the estimated clock — phrased as warnings ("2 Game Changers, above Bracket 2's limit") rather than assigned labels, the stance Archidekt took when it limited its bracket estimator to "1 or 2", "3" or "4 or 5" ([Archidekt brackets](https://archidekt.com/news/11356226)). A "Keep screen on" toggle built on the Screen Wake Lock API, Baseline since 2025-03-31 and supported by Chrome for Android since version 84, would keep the table card and the Watch tab visible; MDN advises showing the lock's state with a way to turn it off, and the lock must be requested again whenever the page becomes visible ([MDN Wake Lock](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/api/screen_wake_lock_api/index.md); [web-features 3.42.0](https://registry.npmjs.org/web-features/-/web-features-3.42.0.tgz)).

The differentiator at the table is a result log, not a life counter. No deck builder in the evidence uses game results, while the trackers that do are praised for it: Playgroup "prompts for game results at the end and has players vote on how the game felt" ([Draftsim life counters](https://draftsim.com/best-mtg-life-counter-app/)), and Playgroup.gg publishes commander win rates across tens of thousands of tracked games ([Playgroup.gg](https://playgroup.gg/commanders/white/all-time)). A one-tap "log this game" on each deck — won or lost, on which turn, against whom and how it felt — stored in the database would close the loop from table to tuning that no surveyed tool closes, and would give the coach and the optimizer real outcomes alongside field percentages. The mulligan trainer is the other table asset worth polishing, because reviewers mark Moxfield's tester down for having no mulligan support ([Draftsim testers](https://draftsim.com/mtg-deck-tester/)). After the double-count fix it needs card images in a 4+3 grid, large Keep and Mulligan buttons with K and M keys, tap-to-bottom for the London mulligan, a land-count readout, no confirmation before a new hand (a complaint about Archidekt's tester) ([Archidekt forum 11522120](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/11522120)), a link back to its deck, and card names without the trailing mana value that makes "Dragonlord Ojutai 5" read like a quantity ([mulligan.html](../webapp/templates/mulligan.html)).

Three Android platform features fit the household's habits and together cost a day or two. A Web Share Target declared in the manifest, supported by Chrome for Android since version 76 but not by iOS, puts the app in Android's share sheet, so a decklist shared from Moxfield, Archidekt, Discord or Notes lands in the existing import preview; it should arrive by POST to avoid URL-length limits and survive a sign-in redirect by stashing the shared text in the session ([MDN share_target](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/progressive_web_apps/manifest/reference/share_target/index.md); [BCD 8.1.5](https://registry.npmjs.org/@mdn/browser-compat-data/-/browser-compat-data-8.1.5.tgz)). Manifest shortcuts, supported by Chrome for Android since version 84 and ignored by iOS, add "Add cards", "Build next", "Search collection" and "Wishlist" to a long-press on the home-screen icon ([MDN shortcuts](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/progressive_web_apps/manifest/reference/shortcuts/index.md)), and `navigator.share()` sends a decklist to another app with a clipboard fallback. The Badging API (no Android support), file handlers (desktop only) and vibration (no iOS) are not worth building ([web-features 3.42.0](https://registry.npmjs.org/web-features/-/web-features-3.42.0.tgz)). Whatever ships, the installed app should keep its standalone look, free of browser chrome — Moxfield users complained as soon as theirs seemed to regress ([nolt /1819](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1819)).

## A four-wave plan puts safety ahead of new features

The waves follow from the evidence: stop the app damaging decks and misstating facts, then make the most-used screen work on a phone with undo underneath it, then fix the heaviest page and the transfer weight, and only then build differentiators. Three dependencies set the order inside the waves. Undo lands before or with the deck-page redesign, because the redesign makes one-tap edits more prominent; the `<dialog>` migration rides with the card-panel rework, so focus and Back are fixed once; and the flow-level UAT goes first, so every later wave is tested by it. Effort labels are estimates for one developer on this Flask, server-rendered, vanilla-JS stack — XS is under half a day, S about a day, M two to four days and L a week or more — and anything touching templates, CSS, JavaScript or the service worker still needs the repo's upgrade and sweep UAT before merge ([webapp/uat/README.md](../webapp/uat/README.md)).

Two guardrails apply to every wave. The first is to keep what works, because each item records a past incident: the Arcane Ledger contrast system and its rule that a status colour always travels with a word, the universal focus ring, 44 px primary controls, `asset()` fingerprinting with the service worker's cache-first and network-first split (guarded by `upgrade.js` since an installed phone painted new markup with old CSS), preview-before-write intake, the honest "estimate / unknown / unreachable" labels, the card panel as a sheet on phones and a drawer on desktop, the Watch tab and the mulligan trainer's decide-first design ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md); audit axe). The second is to change the phone layout in slices the household can absorb. Archidekt's backlash came from moving a frequent action behind one extra tap ([Archidekt mobile overhaul](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)), so each slice should keep the old path reachable for one release, keep Add one tap from the deck with an option to stay open for repeated adds, never bury a primary feature under More, and record taps per core flow against the baseline table above.

## Conclusion

This app's competitive position is the reverse of the market's. Moxfield and Archidekt wrap polished phone interfaces around data models that cannot say where a physical card is, while this app holds the data model their users ask for behind a phone interface written for a terminal. So the highest-return work is presentation, not new analysis — showing facts the engines already compute, at the moment a decision is made — and a self-hosted household app can be best in class on the questions that one-account-per-collection platforms cannot answer by design: which deck holds this copy, who changed it, and how to put it back. It follows that the riskiest code over the next few weeks is not the analysis but the thin layer of templates and JavaScript that writes deck files, which is why validation, undo and flow-level tests come before any visual refresh.

The second shift is in what "tested" means. The existing UAT gate was built after a stylesheet incident and checks how pages look; every damaging defect in this audit lives in how flows behave, and every one passed it. The durable fix for a solo developer is a harness that behaves like a player — replace, add, optimize, mulligan, then read the deck file back — and a 3 KB beacon that measures the real phone instead of an emulator. What desk research cannot supply is the household itself. The table and the shared pool, this app's defining moments of use, carry the thinnest evidence in every note, so before building Wave 3, spend half an hour watching both members build, swap and play.
