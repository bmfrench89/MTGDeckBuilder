# Lead with cards, filter by what's free

> Deep-research report delivered 2026-10-14 (collection pages and filters). The phone audit's screenshots, timing scripts and ground-truth script were working files in the session's scratch space; they are cited by name below but are not in the repo. The spec that acts on this is `docs/spec-collection-rebuild.md`.

The leading collection apps share one shape — a master list of every owned card under user-named containers, one search box, a separate filter screen and a sort — and compete on how far the filter reaches: ManaBox, the consensus phone pick, wins on fast offline search, four colour modes including commander identity and a `Location = Deck` filter, while Archidekt, the strongest web tool, adds removable filter chips, table, image and grid views, real paging and Scryfall syntax over the collection, executed by shipping the whole collection to Scryfall, which slows past 10,000 records. Moxfield accepts the same syntax but ignores its sort keywords in collection mode, TopDecked alone documents typed commands on a phone, and none of the nine surveyed — ManaBox, Archidekt, Moxfield, Deckbox, Dragon Shield, TopDecked, EchoMTG, Delver Lens and the TCGplayer app — can show only the copies that are free to put in a deck, the gap a Moxfield user calls "the largest complaint". This app's Collection page inverts the pattern: on a 412 px phone the first card sits 3.9–4.55 screens down beneath six blocks of admin and intake, its name-only search surfaces 12 of the 53 Dragons in the audited collection, its "Colors" toggles quietly mean "fits this colour identity", its "in a deck" box counts 97 study-only cards as in use, filters reset on Back, and every sort freezes the page for 4.4–6.6 s (17–29 s on a throttled CPU) because of a quadratic MutationObserver that arrived with the accessibility batch merged on 14 October. The fix has two halves. Split the page into three sub-tabs with their own URLs — **Cards** by default, **Add** and **Manage** — so the first card lands on the first screen; then rebuild the Cards filter around one query string that takes plain names for everyone, a Scryfall-compatible subset for power users and collection keywords no rival offers (`free>0`, `decks>=2`, `deck:`, `is:pinned`, `added>=`, `role:`, `for:<deck>`), parsed by one standard-library engine in `scripts/` that the page, the command-line tools and the skill share, run against the app's own data, and honest about missing data instead of returning a misleading zero. The observer guard and the study-deck fix are small enough to ship first. All competitor evidence comes from search-engine extracts — no competitor page or Reddit thread could be opened, several 2026 roundups come from rival scanner vendors, and no native app's filter screen was seen — and the audit ran in phone emulation without card images or prices, so the household's own use should settle what desk research cannot.

## Nine rivals share one shape, and none can find a free copy

### Containers sit on a master list, and intake lives elsewhere

Every app surveyed keeps a master list of everything owned and lets the user lay named containers over it; they differ in what a container means. **ManaBox** splits owned from wanted, with binders for cards you physically own, nameable "after your physical binders", and lists for wishlists and buylists ([ManaBox Collection FAQ](https://www.manabox.app/guides/collection/faq/)). **Deckbox** fixes the containers in advance — "For each selected game, there will be 3 sets automatically created for you": an inventory, a tradelist that is a subset of it, and a wishlist ([Deckbox help](https://deckbox.org/help/start)). **Archidekt** renamed its tags "labels" because "the word 'tag' wasn't super synonymous with a handful of users looking specifically for 'folders' or 'binders'", and gave labels a view of their own ([Archidekt Collection V2++](https://archidekt.com/news/6801001)). **Moxfield** shows binders one at a time beside separate wish-list pages ([Moxfield feedback /1178](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1178); [/1184](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1184)), **Dragon Shield** sorts cards into folders with cover images that track value and win/loss over time ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mtg-scanner-dragon-shield/id1460657155)), and **TopDecked** gives each binder its own sort order and TCG Low, Mid or High price label, though that documentation is about eight years old ([TopDecked tour](https://www.topdecked.com/articles/tour/collection-management-and-binders/)). **Delver Lens** organizes cards into lists that its vendor says "are used as search indexes" ([Delver Lab](https://www.delverlab.com/)), and **the TCGplayer app** drops scans into a temporary list until they are saved to a list in the collection ([TCGplayer App FAQ](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009506407-TCGplayer-App-FAQ)).

Archidekt is the only app whose page layout is well documented, which makes it the clearest model. Its Collection V2++ update, around early 2024, put applied filters "at the top of the page", each "removed with a click"; added an image view, a table with configurable columns and a grid that "will mostly fit more cards on smaller screens"; replaced infinite scroll with pages offering "a page size selector & jump to page options"; and moved the slow "In decks" column into a tab of the card-details overlay "to improve load times" ([Archidekt Collection V2++](https://archidekt.com/news/6801001)). Its CSV importer already lived on a page of its own and its collection options in a Settings menu ([Archidekt importer update](https://archidekt.com/news/5891613); [Archidekt Collections Part II](https://archidekt.com/news/3678345)). Wherever a layout is documented, secondary functions — import, settings, backups, money, even filters — sit on their own page, tab, panel or menu rather than above the list: Deckbox keeps filters behind a "Search and Filter" button at the top right of each list ([Deckbox forum](https://deckbox.org/forum/topics/286)); EchoMTG keeps money in an Earnings tab and per-card detail in an inspector panel beside tables that "can be filtered, searched, and rows can be expanded" ([EchoMTG blog](https://www.echomtg.com/blog/109/echomtg-launches-new-website-to-celebrate-its-10th-year-anniversary-109/); [EchoMTG Earnings tab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCKV4MVwtb4)); and ManaBox puts settings and backups on a Home tab and its set-completion Collector mode behind a button at the bottom of the All collection view ([ManaBox FAQ](https://manabox.app/guides/general/faq/); [ManaBox Collector mode](https://manabox.app/guides/collection/collector-mode/)).

Only ManaBox treats decks as places where cards physically sit. Searching from its All collection view shows "in which decks and binders that card is", a `Location = Deck` filter removes binders from the results, and building a deck from the collection moves the cards out of binders and other decks into it ([ManaBox: decks in the collection](https://manabox.app/guides/decks/collection-decks/)). The others stop short. Archidekt shows each card's decks in an overlay tab and can filter to one named deck, but its staff say a "not in any deck" filter "isn't really possible" because it would load too much data ([Archidekt forum 9981506](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/9981506)); EchoMTG lists deck locations in its inspector ([EchoMTG blog](https://www.echomtg.com/blog/109/echomtg-launches-new-website-to-celebrate-its-10th-year-anniversary-109/)); Moxfield marks owned cards in a deck with a blue check for an exact match and a grey one for a partial match, and goes no further ([Moxfield feedback /1823](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1823)); and a Dragon Shield reviewer reports that inventory and deck lists do not sync, so a card must be scanned twice ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=pt.tscg.mtgmanager&hl=en_US)).

The phone–web split is sharp. Moxfield and Archidekt still have no native app or scanner, and on a phone "some interactions are clunky" ([scanyourmtg comparison](https://www.scanyourmtg.com/compare/archidekt-vs-moxfield/)); Archidekt's 2026 mobile overhaul went to the deck page, not the collection ([Archidekt mobile overhaul](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)), and no 2025–2026 redesign of any web collection page surfaced, so V2++ remains the newest documented model. The table condenses each app's model. Every cell traces to sources cited in this section, and because many of them are feature requests, a listed weak spot shows a gap that existed when someone filed it, not necessarily one that exists today.

### Only Archidekt and Moxfield read Scryfall syntax, each with a catch

Beneath the containers, the apps converge on a search field above the results and a separate filter surface built from form controls, with typed syntax as an optional layer for experts. ManaBox runs one advanced search for plain search, the collection and Collector mode alike, with a colour filter offering four modes — exactly these colours, including them, at most them, and commander colour identity — and a toggle at the top that hides cards not legal in play ([ManaBox Search FAQ](https://www.manabox.app/guides/search/faq/); [ManaBox Collector mode](https://manabox.app/guides/collection/collector-mode/)). TopDecked's filters screen offers dropdowns for mana value, casting cost, colour identity, type and rarity ([TopDecked tour](https://www.topdecked.com/articles/tour/collection-management-and-binders/)); Deckbox builds filters as rows such as `Format==Standard` and `Color==Is Exclusively All of Black, Red`, and its news feed lists separate card-colour and colour-identity filtering ([Deckbox forum](https://deckbox.org/forum/viewtopic.php?id=2052); [Deckbox](https://deckbox.org/)); Delver puts rarity and legality on a filter bar for each list ([Delver Lab](https://www.delverlab.com/)); and Dragon Shield advertises set, colour, mana cost and rarity filters ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mtg-scanner-dragon-shield/id1460657155)). Only Archidekt is documented to show applied filters as removable chips, and no source confirmed whether any native app uses a bottom sheet or a full-screen page for its filters.

Users reward correctness and speed rather than novelty. ManaBox reviewers call its card search "very well done" and its filter options "great" ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460407674/manabox/reviews)), while the complaints elsewhere concern basics: TCGplayer users ask "How can you not have a sorting feature for your collection?" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tcgplayer.tcgplayer&hl=en_US)); Dragon Shield's text search "seems painfully slow" and "you can't even filter your search results to show only cards that you own" ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mtg-scanner-dragon-shield/id1460657155?see-all=reviews)); TopDecked's search worked "about 40% of the time" in reviews from 2019–2022 ([AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/topdecked-mtg-by-topdecked-llc/negative)); and even ManaBox's Oracle-text filter misses cards whose words appear in a different order ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460407674/manabox/reviews)).

Only two apps run Scryfall's query language over the owned collection, and each carries a catch. Archidekt's V2++ made "syntax searching/filtering" work on collections and lets syntax combine with labels through and, or and not ([Archidekt Collection V2++](https://archidekt.com/news/6801001); [Archidekt forum 24950644](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/24950644)), but staff explain that it "requires us to send your entire collection to scryfall, then allow them to filter it down", which "works perfectly fine for small to mid sized collections, but for larger collections (10k+ records) it can slow down significantly" ([Archidekt forum 15983957](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/15983957)). The same design is why staff called "in collection" and "in deck" search terms costly, "since searches go straight to Scryfall's syntax search" ([Archidekt forum 461326](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/461326)): Scryfall knows cards, not anyone's decks.

Moxfield's search accepts the syntax — `f:pioneer` "will show you all cards in your collection that are legal in Pioneer", standing in for the format facet it lacks ([Moxfield feedback /846](https://moxfield.nolt.io/846)) — but drops `order:` sorting "as soon as you select 'Search only cards in your collection'", with staff saying the architecture "is currently being rewritten" and no sign the rewrite shipped ([Moxfield feedback /1287](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1287)); it advertises the feature only through a tooltip, "Pro tip: This search also supports Scryfall syntax", that links to no guide ([Moxfield feedback /1541](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1541)). TopDecked is the one phone app with documented typed commands — AND/OR grouping and shortcuts such as `is:commander` and `is:multi`, with help on its filters screen ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.maritlabs.topdecked.mtg&hl=en_US); [TopDecked 13.0.5 notes](https://marlvel.ai/apps/topdecked-mtg/versions/13.0.5)) — while Delver's "Single Bar Search" has no published grammar and ManaBox, Dragon Shield, Deckbox and TCGplayer document none at all; Deckbox users asking for AND/OR and wildcards concede it "isn't going to give you the extremely advanced results as Scryfall" ([Deckbox forum](https://deckbox.org/forum/viewtopic.php?id=27808)). No native app documents full Scryfall syntax inside the collection.

### Grouping, saved views and "what's free" separate the field

A sort control is table stakes — its absence is TCGplayer's most-cited collection complaint, and Baymard finds that 69% of mobile shopping sites still lack the essential sort types ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-product-list-and-filtering)) — and grouping is the differentiator. ManaBox's Collector mode groups by set, type, colour or rarity, combines groupings and sorts the groups by "Total cards, Collected cards or Percentage of completion" ([ManaBox Collector mode](https://manabox.app/guides/collection/collector-mode/)); Delver separated its group and sort options, then added grouping by artist and a sort by price change ([Delver Lab](https://www.delverlab.com/); [Delver changelog mirror](https://delver-lens-mtg-scanner-beta.apps112.com/)); EchoMTG sorts by date acquired and price change ([EchoMTG API](https://www.echomtg.com/api/)); and Moxfield still carries open requests for "Sorting and Grouping in Collection and Binders" and for a binder "select all" that reaches past the first page ([Moxfield feedback /1357](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1357); [/1158](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1158)). Saved filters barely exist anywhere. Deckbox alone lets users pin a favourite filter set so it stays visible and bookmark filters into a side menu ([Deckbox forum](https://deckbox.org/forum/viewtopic.php?id=32010)); ManaBox can only reuse a search when creating a binder ([App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/manabox-mtg/id1460407674?platform=ipad)); and Moxfield users still ask to save queries and to export only a filtered result ([Moxfield feedback /1688](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1688); [/1485](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1485)).

The capability every app lacks is the one Commander players ask for most. Across Moxfield's feedback board, Archidekt's forum and app-store reviews, about fifteen distinct threads ask which deck holds a copy, how many copies are free, or how to filter to cards in no deck ([Moxfield feedback /1027](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1027); [/841](https://moxfield.nolt.io/841); [Archidekt forum 6308199](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/6308199/1)), and one Moxfield user puts it plainly: "The largest complaint is not to know which cards you own, its which cards are FREE to put in other decks" ([Moxfield feedback /1539](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1539)). Moxfield staff tied the request to a "strict assignment" update for which no shipping evidence was found ([Moxfield feedback /1291](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1291); [/776](https://moxfield.nolt.io/776)), Archidekt's staff call the filter too slow, and the only app found offering anything like it is Leviathan, a small iOS organizer that advertises an "Unused" filter ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6460975870)). The obstacle is scale: comparing every deck against every collection is expensive for a site serving a large user base, but for one household with a dozen decks it is a join this app already computes in `deck_conflicts.available_pool` ([deck_conflicts.py](../scripts/deck_conflicts.py)). That asymmetry, more than any form control, is where this app can lead.

## The Collection page buries its cards under four screens of admin

Measured in phone emulation at 412×915 against a scratch copy of 3,294 unique cards, 4,608 copies and twelve decks, the page puts the household's rarest tasks ahead of its most frequent one (audit results). Its first screen holds only the "Your collection lives here" card — three download buttons, the start-up report and the start of a "Change a card's count" form — and the browser's Largest Contentful Paint element is that card's explanatory paragraph, not a card (phone first screen). Below it come the card-database status, the reconcile prompt shown after an upload, "Add a batch" with its 275 px paste box, "Add one card" with "Merge an export", and New arrivals: six blocks of administration and intake before the search box ([collection.html](../webapp/templates/collection.html)). **The first card sits at 3,572 px, 3.9 screens down, and once the Recent changes list fills, as it does on the live server after any edit, at 4,162 px, or 4.55 screens** (4.9 counting only the 850 px the fixed tab bar leaves visible) (recent-changes screenshot). The "Browse your collection" heading has no anchor and nothing links to it, the page runs to 219,277 px or 239.6 screens, and the only value view, "Most valuable cards", sits 239 screens down below the whole grid.

The controls are thinner than the data behind them. Search is a case-insensitive substring match on the name alone, run on every keystroke ([collection.js](../webapp/static/collection.js)). The five colour buttons, labelled "Colors" and drawn as bare letters with no `aria-pressed`, actually test whether a card's colour identity fits within the selection, and colourless and unknown cards pass every pick ([app.py](../webapp/app.py)) — a sound Commander question, "does it fit a deck of these colours?", under a label a player will read as "contains this colour". The type select matches one primary type per card, the role select offers five roles, the four sorts have no direction toggle, and there is no grouping, no list view and no saved state: a reload or a Back press resets everything, and a search that finds nothing shows "0 cards" with no message and no reset (zero-result screenshot). The "in a deck" checkbox reads every deck file, study copies included, although the conflict engine treats study decks as committing nothing, so **97 of the 655 cards it flags as in use sit only in study lists** ([deck_conflicts.py](../scripts/deck_conflicts.py)).

Meanwhile the page ignores data the app already holds: subtypes on 2,008 cards across 235 distinct types, actual colours and mana costs, dates added on 528 cards, mana production on 411, oracle-derived flags on 993, power on 1,654, pins, power tags and, above all, free copies — 2,904 cards with at least one copy outside every sleeved deck, 170 in a deck with spare copies, and 15 committed beyond the copies owned (groundtruth.py; [mtglib.py](../scripts/mtglib.py)). A better search already exists, too: `/api/collection/search` ranks prefix matches first, can restrict results to a deck's identity and answers in about 10 ms, but only the deck's add-card picker uses it ([app.py](../webapp/app.py); audit timings).

Three everyday questions show the cost. Finding Sol Ring means scrolling about 3.5 screens to the search box and typing "sol", which returns 22 cards alphabetically, Sol Ring thirteenth behind Absolute Grace, Desolate Mire and Isolated Chapel, while "solring", "ring sol" and "ur dragon" return nothing (search screenshot). Counting Dragons fails outright: "dragon" returns 24 names, only 12 of them Dragons, while the audited collection holds 53 cards with the Dragon subtype, so **41 Dragons are unreachable by any search or filter** — on the one page where the project's first rule, "count the pool; never spot-check staples", most needs to work ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). And "all my free green ramp" has no answer. Tapping G and Ramp returns 94 cards, 35 of them colourless rocks or cards with no type data, and misses the 6 multicolour green ramp cards; ticking "in a deck" leaves 44, and the player must subtract by hand to reach 50 cards in no deck, which still misses cards with spare copies (G + Ramp screenshot). Re-running the audit's ground-truth script shows why one colour button cannot settle it: **70 cards have a free copy if "green" means "fits a mono-green deck", colourless rocks included, but 47 do if it means "ramp that is actually green"** (groundtruth.py). Both are right answers, so the colour mode must be visible and choosable — the point of ManaBox's four modes.

### Every sort freezes the page, a regression from the accessibility batch

Rendering, not filtering, is the cost. The server sends all 3,294 tiles at once — 11,003 DOM elements in 1.23 MB of HTML, of which the grid is 98.5% — and the gzip compression shipped in the refresh's Wave 0 cuts the transfer to 118 KB without reducing the work the phone must do ([app.py](../webapp/app.py); audit results). Narrowing filters are fast, with handlers of 2–8 ms reaching the next frame in under 80 ms, but clearing a search takes 545 ms, or 1.8 s at four-times CPU throttling, and **every sort takes 4.4–6.6 s, and 17.7–29.2 s throttled, though the sort handler itself runs in 44–90 ms**. The audit traced the freeze three ways: Long Animation Frame entries attributed 3.6–4.8 s of script to the sort's task after the handler returned, a bare reorder of the grid with none of the sort code still took 5.1 s, and disabling `MutationObserver` brought the same sorts down to 0.43–0.61 s (collaudit_sortdiag.js; collaudit_moctl.js). The culprit is a document-wide observer in `cardpanel.js`: a sort re-appends all 3,294 tiles, and for each one the observer re-scans the whole grid for `[data-card]` elements, about 3,294 × 3,294 or 10.8 million checks per sort ([cardpanel.js](../webapp/static/cardpanel.js)). It arrived with the accessibility batch merged on 14 October, whose message says it "covers lists drawn later by script" so that a keyboard can reach every card name ([commit 4c4c53e](https://github.com/bmfrench89/MTGDeckBuilder/commit/4c4c53ee2856db9daecc914078e32e104fbf5538)); scanning only each added node, plus the node itself when it carries `data-card`, keeps that benefit at linear cost.

The "Most played (EDHREC)" sort makes it worse: choosing it fired 44 POSTs carrying 3,250 names at Scryfall within 57 ms and re-sorts the grid as each batch lands, and its loader swallows failures with an empty `catch`, without the retry and pacing that the repo's own image notes credit with preventing 429 rate-limit cascades ([collection.js](../webapp/static/collection.js); [docs/card-images.md](../docs/card-images.md)). The rest is phone ergonomics. The 359 px controls card is not sticky, so from the end of the grid the filters lie 235 screens back up; 45% of tile captions are truncated on the phone and 71% on desktop; a tile shows only its picture, a quantity badge and eventually a popularity chip, never its decks, free copies or price; unresolved images show a broken-image glyph, which the repo's image notes say a loader should never show; the grid adds 3,294 tab stops; the card panel shows "13× owned" but never how many copies are free; and offline the page is unreachable ([docs/card-images.md](../docs/card-images.md); [sw.js](../webapp/static/sw.js)). What already works should survive the rebuild: no horizontal overflow, 44 px tap targets, 16 px inputs that stop iOS zooming the page, and narrowing filters that respond in under 80 ms ([app.css](../webapp/static/app.css)).

## Three sub-tabs put the first card on the first screen

The design evidence favours separating the collection the household uses from the machinery that maintains it. NN/g distinguishes in-page tabs, which hold similar content and keep people in place, from navigation tabs, which hold different content and move people to another page, warning that "mixing in-page and navigation tabs within one tab control will disorient users" and that "the fewer tabs, the better" ([NN/g: Tabs, Used Right](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/)); Material 3 reserves secondary tabs for content areas that need a second level and, as extracted, advises against more than four ([Material 3 tabs](https://m3.material.io/components/tabs/guidelines)). The closest precedent is Apple's retreat in Photos: after users rejected iOS 18's single long scroll, which mixed the photo library with albums and utilities, iOS 26 brought tabs back, and Craig Federighi announced that "Photos now features separate tabs for Library and for Collections" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/09/after-user-backlash-apple-brings-back-tabs-to-the-photos-app-in-ios-26/)). Apple also paid a visible cost — iCloud sync status now takes several taps instead of sitting under the grid ([Gadget Hacks](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/news/why-photos-in-ios-26-finally-makes-sense-again/)) — which argues for surfacing pending work as a tab badge, something Material limits to four characters. Attention data points the same way, with 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% within the first two screenfuls in NN/g's 2018 desktop eye-tracking ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)), and Baymard warns that oversized mobile banners push navigation off the first screen ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-category-page)).

The page should therefore become three navigation sub-tabs, each with its own URL: **Cards**, the default, at `/collection`; **Add**, for every way cards come in; and **Manage**, for everything that keeps the record honest. Three labels fit a 360 px screen with room to spare and stay within every guideline above; a fourth would fit too, but nothing on the page is used often enough to earn one. Built as ordinary links, with the current tab marked by two cues such as an underline and a heavier weight rather than colour alone, the tabs keep Back, reload and bookmarks working and give the manifest shortcuts and Android share target already planned in the refresh a real address to land on ([spec-ux-refresh.md](../docs/spec-ux-refresh.md)). Two alternatives deserve a hearing. A single page that lists cards first and folds administration into a "Manage" disclosure, the wording of the current spec, is cheaper, but it still ships the admin markup and gives intake no address, leaving it either above the cards or below a long list; in-page tabs without URLs would mix navigation with view switching, the error NN/g names, and break Back. Add deserves its own tab rather than a place under the bottom bar's More menu because this household adds cards in dated batches — 528 dated cards in the audit's copy — and Archidekt's 2026 overhaul showed what burying a frequent action costs, when users found adding twenty cards through a drawer "tiresome" until a keep-open toggle arrived ([Archidekt mobile overhaul](https://archidekt.com/news/22633051)).

Inside Cards, the list must own the first screen. The page header and sub-tab bar can scroll away and return on an upward swipe, which Material allows for tabs and NN/g describes as the usual mobile header pattern ([Material 3 tabs](https://m3.material.io/components/tabs/guidelines); [NN/g: Sticky Headers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sticky-headers/)), leaving pinned only a slim search bar that holds the field, a "Filters (2)" button and a Sort button. Beneath it sit one chip row and one summary line that doubles as the result count, then the list — roughly 125 px of controls where today's controls card takes 359 px, which by our estimate puts the first row in the top third of the first screen. Cards needs no further tabs: Spotify replaced its tabbed library with combinable filter chips and a sort control above the list ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/spotify-your-library-ui-update-dynamic-filters)), and Apple presents search scopes as a segmented control under the search field ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10052/)). The location scopes — All, Free, In decks, Pinned, Shared — therefore belong in a single-select chip set, kept apart from the multi-select facet chips because Material forbids mixing the two kinds in one set ([Material 3 chips](https://m3.material.io/components/chips/guidelines)).

Two moves finish the reorganization. The "Change a card's count" form should become a quantity stepper inside the card panel, where the player already is when they notice a wrong count, saving at once with the Undo toast that deck edits gained in Wave 1 of the refresh, and the panel's ownership line should read, for example, "4 owned · 3 in decks · 1 free" instead of "13× owned". And the safety the app already has — one server record, a ledger of every change, merge-only uploads and daily backups ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)) — deserves one quiet line at the foot of Cards, such as "Every change is logged · last backup 06:00 · History", because data loss drives the category's most damaging reviews; one ManaBox user had to rescan about 10,000 cards ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=skilldevs.com.manabox&hl=en_US)).

### One string should carry every filter

The single most useful design decision is to make one query string the source of truth for everything the filter does. Chips write terms into it and typed terms become chips; the URL carries it; a saved view stores it; and the command line and the skill accept the same text — so a player who never types syntax gets point-and-click, a player who does gets precision, and the two can never disagree. mtg-grimoire, a 2026 open-source collection app, shows the loop working: each parsed term draws a chip that can be removed or negated, an `or` group draws one chip "labelled with its source text" ([mtg-grimoire #930](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/930)), and plain free text stays an instant name match while typed terms wait 300 ms with the last results kept on screen ([mtg-grimoire #629](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/629)). Forms stay the front door. ManaBox, the most praised app, has no typed syntax at all, and Microsoft's FaThumb study found keyword search worked when the target's name was known and facets "more effective and strongly preferred" otherwise ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=145435)); the syntax is the representation beneath the forms, not a test the household must pass.

Where Scryfall defines a behaviour, the app should copy it exactly, because Scryfall's semantics are what Moxfield, Archidekt and the Commander tutorials teach. Terms combine with an implicit AND, `or` and parentheses group alternatives, a leading hyphen negates, `!` demands an exact name and slashes wrap a regular expression ([Scryfall syntax](https://scryfall.com/docs/syntax)); AND binds tighter than OR, which an independent implementer confirms is Scryfall's precedence and Cockatrice's grammar reverses ([mtg-grimoire #930](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/930); [Cockatrice](https://github.com/Cockatrice/Cockatrice/blob/master/libcockatrice_filters/libcockatrice/filters/filter_string.cpp)). Colour needs the most care. Scryfall's own result pages translate a colour colon as "at least" (`-c:w` reads "the colors are not ≥ W") but an identity colon as "within" (`identity:uw` reads "color identity ≤ UW") ([Scryfall result page](https://scryfall.com/search?q=identity%3Auw+-color%3Aw+-color%3Am)), a coverage reading Scryfall adopted years ago so that "id:wug will find all cards that you can put in a white/blue/green commander deck" ([Scryfall blog](https://scryfall.com/blog/color-identity-searches-now-check-coverage-7)). Lucky Paper notes that for identity the colon, "conveniently for commander", works like `<=` ([Lucky Paper](https://luckypaper.co/articles/searching-with-scryfall-magic-at-your-fingertips/)), and Commander's Herald teaches `id<=wgu` for everything playable in Bant against `id=wgu is:commander` for Bant's commanders ([Commander's Herald](https://commandersherald.com/the-ultimate-scryfall-primer/)). MTG Arena instead reads a colon on colours as an exact match ([MTG Arena Zone](https://mtgazone.com/advanced-search/)), so players arrive with conflicting habits, and a plain-English summary under the results — the line Scryfall prints — settles which reading applied. In the form, the colour buttons should carry an explicit mode — Fits (identity within, the Commander default), Contains and Exactly — because the green-ramp question has two honest answers, 70 and 47, that differ only in that mode.

### Collection keywords give the household answers no rival has

On top of Scryfall's vocabulary the app should add the words only a deck-aware household app can answer. No mainstream collection site documents keywords for free copies, deck use or date added; the nearest precedents are CubeCobra's `status:` values for cube cards, `owned`, `proxied` and `borrowed` among them, and two small 2026 viewers of ManaBox exports that add `qty>=2`, `binder:` and `loc:` ([CubeCobra values.ne](https://github.com/dekkerglen/CubeCobra/blob/master/packages/utils/nearley/values.ne); [manabox-viewer](https://github.com/AleRosae/manabox-viewer); [mtg-collection-search](https://github.com/HunterEScheel/mtg-collection-search)). Each keyword below follows Scryfall's key-operator-value shape so it composes with everything else; `decks>0` should be the canonical form, with `in:deck` only an alias, because `in:` is already a Scryfall printing keyword; and keywords with no data behind them, such as foil, condition or binder, should wait until the data model stores them. The support column says how well the app's own data can answer each one.

### Unknown data must never look like zero

Honesty is where an engine running on the app's own data can most clearly beat the field, and the repo's rules already say how. Every condition should come out true, false or unknown — unknown when the data is absent, as on a name-only row with no types or a card whose mana production was never enriched — and unknown rows should be excluded and counted in the summary ("3 skipped: no type data yet"), not passed silently the way untyped cards pass every colour pick today. That is the project's empty-versus-absent rule applied to search ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)), and mtg-grimoire's bug report shows the cost of ignoring it: a null that "meant two things" made a fallback fire on every vanilla card ([mtg-grimoire #498](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/498)). On the committed snapshot, which carries no supertypes, `t:legendary` should therefore report "unknown for every card", never "0 matches".

Errors need the same care. Scryfall ignores a bad term with an "Invalid expression … Unknown keyword" notice and runs the rest ([Scryfall result page](https://scryfall.com/search?as=grid&order=released&page=3&q=ct%3Atownsfolk+-e%3Auh&unique=cards)), while one developer found that a syntax error from Scryfall's API "looked the same as a genuine zero-match result" ([decklist-tools #251](https://github.com/kapunga/decklist-tools/issues/251)). Here a misspelled `rol:ramp` read as a name would return nothing and imply the household owns no ramp, so an unknown keyword must produce a warning that names it and suggests a fix, never a silent zero. Two more traps have known fixes: Archidekt's built-in "smart filters" skipped "the first half" of an OR query, so the app's own conditions must wrap the user's query in parentheses before joining it ([Archidekt forum 10580830](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/10580830)), and a dangling `or`, open quote or empty `key:` left mid-typing should be closed or dropped rather than reported as an error ([mtg-grimoire #930](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/930)). Oracle text is the one popular operator the app cannot answer today, since enrichment reads card text to derive flags but does not keep it ([carddb.py](../scripts/carddb.py)); until the server, which can reach Scryfall, stores that text, `o:` should say so and suggest `role:`.

Help should be generated, not written. mtg-grimoire builds its syntax panel "from the parser's own keyword table — so it cannot advertise a keyword nothing parses" ([mtg-grimoire #498](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/498)), the principle this repo already enforces for its tools through `tests/test_tool_contract.py`. Archidekt's "i" icon beside its search box links straight to Scryfall's reference ([Archidekt forum](https://archidekt.com/forum/thread/2065086/1)), whereas Moxfield offers only its bare tooltip and Arena's in-game tooltip omits negation, which MTG Arena Zone calls "the most important point" ([MTG Arena Zone](https://mtgazone.com/advanced-search/)). A "?" beside the search field, listing each keyword with one runnable example drawn from the parser's own table, closes that gap.

## Mechanics and plumbing decide whether it feels instant

### A sticky bar, a live count and removable chips

Baymard's mobile filtering research reads like an acceptance checklist for the Cards tab. Its most common failures are repeated trips between the list and a separate filter screen, no overview of applied filters (28–32% of sites), facets that accept only one value (14–15%), key filters left unpromoted (61%), incomplete sorting (69% of mobile sites) and combinations that silently return nothing ([Baymard mobile benchmark](https://baymard.com/mcommerce-usability/benchmark/mobile-page-types/filtering-options); [applied filters](https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-design-applied-filters); [multiple values](https://baymard.com/blog/allow-applying-of-multiple-filter-values); [promoted filters](https://baymard.com/blog/promoting-product-filters); [filter UI](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-filter-ui)). Its recommended answer — a clearly labelled, sticky filter button opening a full-screen overlay or bottom sheet, an explicit "Show X results" button whose count updates "so users know what they're committing to", and applied filters shown above the list — maps directly onto the app. On a phone the Filters button should open a full-height `<dialog>` built with `showModal()`, because eight to ten facets overflow Material's half-height modal sheet ([Material 3 bottom sheets](https://m3.material.io/components/bottom-sheets/guidelines)). Facets run from general to specific — location, colour identity with its mode, type and subtype, mana value, role, power tags, rarity and set, estimated price, date added — each value showing its match count, and the long lists, 235 subtypes and every set, get a search box inside the sheet ([Baymard: long filter lists](https://baymard.com/research-articles/allow-search-for-long-filter-options-lists)). A sticky footer pairs Reset with "Show 70 cards", disabled and reading "No cards match" at zero. On desktop the same panel becomes a persistent side panel, the way Card Kingdom's advanced search "stays visible on the left side of your screen as you browse" ([Card Kingdom](https://blog.cardkingdom.com/using-the-advanced-search-tool-like-a-pro/)), and outside the sheet, where the list stays visible, chips should apply instantly, since NN/g's guidance is that batch versus instant application stops mattering when "the results can be computed and displayed quickly enough" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-filters/)).

The sheet must behave like a proper dialog. Opening it pushes a history entry so Android's back gesture closes the sheet instead of leaving the page; focus moves to the sheet's title on open and back to the Filters button on close; Escape and a visible × both close it; and it never stacks a second sheet on top of itself ([NN/g: Bottom Sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/); [W3C APG dialog](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/); [Baymard: Back button](https://baymard.com/research-articles/back-button-expectations)). The summary line under the chips should be the page's one `role="status"` region, announcing a line such as "70 cards · identity within G · role ramp · free copies > 0" without moving focus, which WCAG 4.1.3 treats as exactly this kind of status message ([W3C Understanding 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)). Chips need `aria-pressed`, a check icon as well as a fill, text names for the colour buttons, and the whole chip as the target for removing it — 44 px under the repo's `--target` token, well above WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px floor ([W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). And a search that finds nothing must say why. Baymard finds 88% of mobile sites offer no context-aware help when a search returns nothing ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/no-results-page)), so the empty state should list the active filters as removable chips, say how many cards match without the narrowest filter where that is cheap to compute, offer a "did you mean" for names, which Python's standard `difflib` can supply, link to a search of all cards on Scryfall, and, when the card is on the wishlist, name the deck that wants it.

### Sort, group, view and save

Sort should be its own control that shows its current value, because filters set hard boundaries and sorting soft ones ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/category-specific-sorting)). It needs a direction toggle and the options a Commander household actually uses — name, mana value, colour and type; copies owned, free copies and decks using the card; date added, estimated price and field popularity — plus relevance, with prefix matches first as `/api/collection/search` already ranks them, whenever a name is typed. Grouping should work independently of sorting, as Delver separated them and Moxfield's deck views do ([Moxfield wiki](https://github.com/moxfield/moxfield-public/wiki/Features)), with collapsible headers that carry counts; where ManaBox shows per-group completion, a household that does not collect sets needs owned and free counts instead, so grouping by type answers "how many Dragons, and how many are free?" at a glance. On a phone the default view should be a list of 48–56 px rows — a small thumbnail, the name, quantity and free count, mana symbols, the type line and deck chips — because 45% of grid captions are truncated and tiles depend on image hotlinks that fail on a weak connection; the image grid stays one tap away for recognizing cards by art, both choices are remembered for each viewer, and Baymard's rule that every attribute shown in a row must also be filterable checks the row's design ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/have-filters-for-list-item-info)).

Saved views would put the app ahead of every rival. A saved view is a named query string — "Free green ramp", "Unsleeved Dragons", "New this month" — stored in the collection database so both household members see it; only Deckbox offers anything like it, and Moxfield users are still asking ([Deckbox forum](https://deckbox.org/forum/viewtopic.php?id=32010); [Moxfield feedback /1688](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1688)). A "Download these results" link on any result answers Moxfield's request to export a filtered subset ([Moxfield feedback /1485](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1485)).

### One parser in scripts/ serves the page, the CLIs and the skill

The engine belongs in `scripts/`, not in the browser: a new standard-library module with a tokenizer built on `re`, a small recursive-descent parser using Scryfall's precedence, and an evaluator that compiles the parsed query into a Python test applied to each card. It can follow the dict-in, set-out pattern of `oracle_flags`, importing only `mtglib` for name normalization and the basics rule while callers pass in each card's deck use, free count, pins and power tags, which keeps the repo's dependency rule intact ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Evaluating in Python makes regular expressions free — mtg-grimoire refused them because SQLite would need a REGEXP function registered on every connection ([mtg-grimoire #930](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/930)) — and mtg-grimoire's other lesson, one shared filter function for search, collection and wishlist so a new condition lands everywhere at once ([mtg-grimoire #498](https://github.com/Msgaihede/mtg-grimoire/pull/498)), matches this repo's rule that the web app imports the scripts rather than duplicating them. The same function then backs a `--query` flag on `analyze_collection.py`, so the skill can count the pool with the exact text a player types; the tool contract and its test must explain the new flag. Licences decide how much code can be borrowed: Penny Dreadful's tokenizer (MIT) and CubeCobra's grammar (Apache-2.0) can be adapted with attribution, while Cockatrice (GPL-2.0) and mtg-grimoire (AGPL-3.0) serve as design references only ([Penny Dreadful](https://github.com/PennyDreadfulMTG/Penny-Dreadful-Tools/blob/master/find/search.py); [CubeCobra](https://github.com/dekkerglen/CubeCobra/blob/master/packages/utils/nearley/cardFilters.ne)), which makes a clean-room parser of roughly 300 lines, by the research's estimate, the practical route. Hermetic tests should pin the behaviours this report measured: "sol" ranks Sol Ring first, "solring" and "ur dragon" find their cards, `t:dragon` counts 53 on the audit's data, `id<=g role:ramp free>0` and `id>=g role:ramp free>0` return 70 and 47, untyped rows are counted as skipped, and an unknown keyword warns instead of returning zero.

Evaluation should run on the server, which sends results in batches. It renders the first 100 matching rows with a real `?n=200` "Load more" link that script upgrades to append rows in place, rewriting the URL with `history.replaceState` so that Back from a card lands at the same depth — "a technical detail that's critical to get right" in Baymard's words, and one 13% of sites still miss ([Baymard: items loaded](https://baymard.com/blog/number-of-items-loaded-by-default); [Baymard: return to the same place](https://baymard.com/blog/return-same-place)) — and it shows "Showing 100 of 3,294", because a "Load more" that hides the total leaves users with "no sense of when they would reach the end" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/alternatives-pagination-listing-pages/)). One hundred rows of roughly ten elements each keeps the page near Lighthouse's 800-node warning, by our estimate, rather than at today's 11,003 elements ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/dom-size)), and `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size` on each row lets the browser skip rendering off-screen rows — initial rendering fell from 232 ms to 30 ms in web.dev's demo — while find-in-page and the accessibility tree keep working, which JavaScript virtualization breaks ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility)).

The case for keeping everything in the browser deserves its strongest form. ManaBox and Delver search on the device, client-side filtering needs no round trip and works offline, and Moxfield users complain of a "round trip to the API on every key stroke" ([Moxfield feedback /1181](https://moxfield.nolt.io/1181)). But a second parser in JavaScript would drift from the Python one the skill and command line use, and sending the whole collection to the page is exactly what made this one heavy. Debouncing typed syntax, keeping the last results on screen while the next arrive, and the offline saved copies already planned for the refresh answer most of the objection; the same plan's web-vitals beacon will measure tap responsiveness (Interaction to Next Paint) on the real phones, and if it shows round trips hurting, a compact in-browser index of names alone is the fallback ([web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp); [spec-ux-refresh.md](../docs/spec-ux-refresh.md)). One smaller server change removes the Scryfall burst: because enrichment already fetches each card's Scryfall object, it can store the card's EDHREC rank at the same time and turn "Most played" into an ordinary server sort — our inference, not a tested change.

## Conclusion

The market's hardest collection problem is this app's cheapest. Archidekt and Moxfield cannot say which copies are free because comparing every deck against every collection is expensive across a whole user base; for one household with a dozen decks it is a join the app already runs. So the way to beat the field is not a prettier filter sheet — the sheet, chips and sorts are parts borrowed from Baymard, Archidekt and ManaBox — but a vocabulary only a deck-aware, single-household tool can afford: `free>0`, `deck:`, `is:shared`, `for:<deck>`. Making that vocabulary one query string also turns the Collection page from a gallery into an instrument of the project's grounding rules: the text that filters the page is the text that counts the pool for the skill, so "how many Dragons do we own, and how many are free?" gets one answer whether the page, the command line or the coach is asked.

The second lesson concerns process. The sort freeze came from a well-meant accessibility fix and passed every gate, all fourteen UAT flows included, because none of them sorts 3,294 tiles and times the result ([spec-ux-refresh.md](../docs/spec-ux-refresh.md)); the earlier audit's warning — that damaging bugs live in flows the sweep never runs — came true again the same day. The rebuild should therefore arrive with timed flows (search, sort, filter, Back) in the harness and the real-phone beacon switched on, because the remaining questions — whether either member ever types syntax, whether a server round trip feels instant on their phones, and whether Cards, Add and Manage match how they actually work — are ones only the household's own use can answer.
